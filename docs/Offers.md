## DIRECTION 1: Decide the positioning (this comes before writing)

### Your core angle
You are **not** writing:
- “How I’m smart”
- “How I got lucky”
- “How to get a job fast”

You **are** writing:
> “How recruiters decide whom to message, and how I aligned my profile to those signals.”

Write this sentence at the top of a doc. Everything must support it.

If a paragraph doesn’t explain a *signal*, delete it.

---

## DIRECTION 2: Collect raw evidence (30–45 minutes)

Create a folder called:
**`linkedin-inbound-proof`**

Now do this:

1. Screenshot or copy:
   - Recruiter messages
   - Hiring manager messages
   - Founder messages

2. For each message, note in a sheet:
   - Company
   - Role
   - Why they likely contacted you (guess honestly)

Example:
> Deloitte – AI Strategy – Keyword match + leadership phrasing

This step prevents vague writing later.

---

## DIRECTION 3: Reverse-engineer patterns (most important step)

Answer these questions on paper:

### 1. What keywords repeat across messages?
Examples:
- GenAI
- LLM
- Python
- Vision-Language
- Technical Lead

These are **search triggers**.

---

### 2. What seniority do they assume you are?
Junior? Lead? IC?

If recruiters message you for leadership roles, that tells you:
- Your wording implies ownership and delivery, not learning

Write this down explicitly.

---

### 3. What did they NOT ask about?
Usually:
- Grades
- Certificates
- Online courses

This is a strong insight. Most candidates focus on the wrong things.

---

## DIRECTION 4: Structure the blog (do not improvise)

Use this exact structure:

### 1. Opening (credibility first)
- 3–5 anonymized recruiter excerpts
- One sentence explaining the pattern

No long intro.

---

### 2. Section: “What recruiters actually saw”
Sub-sections:
- Headline clarity
- Proof of execution
- Narrative consistency

Each sub-section must:
- Explain the signal
- Show how you implemented it

---

### 3. Section: Market reality check
Explain:
- Why GenAI hiring is noisy
- Why positioning matters more than volume

This shows maturity and honesty.

---

### 4. Section: Conversion matters
Explain:
- Fast replies
- Clean follow-ups
- Professional tone

This connects your follow-up messages to outcomes.

---

### 5. Section: Checklist
Concrete, copy-able items only.

No theory.

---

### 6. Closing insight
One sharp truth about hiring signals.

No motivational ending.

---

## DIRECTION 5: Write in “engineering mode”

Rules while writing:
- No adjectives unless they add information
- No claims without examples
- No “I believe” statements

Replace:
> “I optimized my profile”

With:
> “I reduced my headline to one role and four keywords.”

---

## DIRECTION 6: Add one visual element (important)

Choose **one**:
- Annotated LinkedIn profile screenshot
- Before/after headline comparison
- Flow diagram: Recruiter → Profile → Message

This increases trust and retention.

---

## DIRECTION 7: Publish strategically

### Where to publish:
- Medium or personal blog (long-form)
- LinkedIn (thread or article)

### How:
1. Publish long-form first
2. Create a LinkedIn post summarizing 3 insights
3. Link to the blog

Do **not** dump the whole blog on LinkedIn.

---

## DIRECTION 8: Invite discussion, not praise

End your LinkedIn post with:
> If you’re not getting recruiter messages, which part of your profile do you think is unclear?

This drives engagement and signals authority.

---

## Final reality check (important)

This will work **only if**:
- You focus on *signals*, not success
- You stay specific
- You don’t over-promise results

Your edge is clarity, not hype.
